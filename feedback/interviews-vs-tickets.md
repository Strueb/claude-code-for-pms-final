# Interviews vs support tickets: where they disagree

Sources: four handler interviews (2-5 Sep 2026, console redesign research) and 147 support tickets (29 Jun to 7 Sep 2026). See `handler-interviews-summary.md` and `support-tickets-summary.md`. Handler and responder names appear; keep this out of wide channels.

Only one of the four interviewees (Ambrose) filed any tickets, so the two sources mostly hear from different people. Tickets are self-selected, and the interviews were scoped to the console redesign with set questions, so absence from either source isn't proof.

## Where they disagree
- **Which symptom is bigger.** Interviews: "gone before I could answer" was the headline (3 of 4), quiet stretches secondary (2 of 4). Tickets: quiet is twice as big (30 tickets against 15).
- **How many people are affected.** "Gone too fast" is spread wide: 11 of the 12 ticket-filers reported it, one or two tickets each. "Quiet" is concentrated: 4 handlers filed all 30 tickets, mostly repeat follow-ups. The interviews match the first pattern; the tickets reveal the second.
- **Tone.** Interviewees were calm (Aunt Dot didn't mind the quiet weeks; Kip said he's "not the person to ask"). Ticket-filers escalated: "He has started asking me every day whether something is broken. I do not know what to tell him."
- **Maintenance scheduling.** Halloran said it has "gotten smarter" about avoiding bad weeks. The tickets have 4 complaints about maintenance booked on the city marathon day or reminders arriving after the service date. Halloran is one person, but it is a direct contradiction.
- **Who is heard.** Aunt Dot and Kip filed no tickets. Farlight's and The Undertow's handlers filed many but weren't interviewed. The interviews and tickets cover opposite halves of the four frozen-out responders.

## Loud in the interviews, rare in the tickets
- **Alerts that help the handler notice a live callout.** Aunt Dot wanted to be notified herself; Ambrose wanted live callouts "harder to miss". No ticket asks for either. Kip's different-sound-per-responder request appears twice, as "same alert sound for everything".
- **Dark mode.** Kip begged for it; it appears in only 4 tickets (3%), one from each of 4 handlers.
- **Small text.** Raised by 2 of 4 interviewees; only 2 tickets, both about status badges.
- **Uneven workload across one handler's responders.** Kip's side-by-side cards story has no ticket equivalent. Tickets only report the quiet side.

## All over the tickets, absent from the interviews
- **Filters.** 16 tickets from 11 of 12 handlers (wrong computer, a colleague's filters, no way to clear them all, easy-to-miss badge). Only Ambrose mentioned filters, and he was glad it shipped. Working notes call this noise.
- **Console feature requests.** 34 tickets: screen reader support, keyboard shortcuts, printing, handover notes, time zones, leave status, exports, sign-outs, slow Mondays. None came up in the interviews.
- **Phone reliability.** Duplicate notifications (3), unreliable on older phones (1), accidental decline (1), out of signal (1). Nobody mentioned these in the interviews.
- **Supply beyond Halloran's three.** 20 of the 26 Supply tickets (back-ordered masks, gloves wearing out, wrong size, gear delivered to the wrong office, and so on). Halloran's three complaints do match tickets nearly word for word: the requisition priority field, failure reports with no reply, and the "plate" search returning forty results. He filed none himself.
- **Billing.** 2 tickets where an invoice lists a responder twice.
- **A second tier of quiet responders.** Corporal Ashgrove and Halfmoon report a smaller dip; no interviewee covers them.

## What it means
The interviews give the human description of "gone too fast" and of the two quietest responders. The tickets give the scale, the second tier and the escalation, plus a lot of ordinary console demand nobody raised in the interviews.
