How Dispatch decides who to ask, and what happens when they don't say yes

The routing code in 00-rook/code/dispatch-routing/, as it stands after 4.2 (12 Aug 2026).

A step in the routing Changed in 4.2, or where a miss is treated like a turn-down An end state
The same flow as text
  1. A callout arrives with a region, a time, a location and the skills it needs.
  2. Dispatch finds everyone marked available in that region now (availability.py). It does not change anyone's availability.
  3. It scores each of them: 0.60 × travel-time closeness + 0.25 × recent-acceptance score + 0.15 × share of needed skills they have (routing.py, weights in config.py), and sorts best first. Nobody is removed.
  4. It takes the next responder on the list, pings their phone and waits up to 60 seconds (offer.py).
  5. If they take it, their score goes up 0.08 and the callout is filled.
  6. If they turn it down, their score goes down 0.12 and Dispatch moves on.
  7. If they don't answer in 60 seconds, the offer is withdrawn from their phone and recorded as missed. Their score goes down 0.12, the same as a turn-down, and Dispatch moves on.
  8. If there is nobody left, the callout is unfilled: the code returns nobody, with no retry and no alert.

Drawn from reading the code, not from running it. availability.py, the device calls in offer.py and the travel-time estimate are placeholders in this copy, so what they do inside is not shown. Scores are held in history.py: between 0 and 1, starting at 0.5. A responder's score only changes when they are pinged.
